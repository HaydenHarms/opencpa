## Optional suggestions

1. **Key-position skew (batch-level cue).** Options are sorted ascending and the key is usually a middle value. Across 64 versions: A 9, B 29, C 23, D 3. A test-wise student who never picks D, and usually picks B or C, gains an edge. Where it is natural, put the correct value at an extreme: for example, make "all three effects combined" or "omits one adjustment" the middle values in some versions.
2. **far-accounting-errors-0012 v0 and v2.** The shares lose value, so the "write down losses only (LCM)" misconception gives the key. Consider a gain in one of these two versions (v1 and v3 already have gains) or accept it as a variant mix.
3. **far-subsequent-events-0018.** A reviewer could cite ASC 606-10-32-42/43 (transaction-price changes recognized in the period of change). The Type I treatment under ASC 855 is the mainstream exam answer, since the settlement gives evidence about a condition that existed at year-end. The explanation should cite both topics and say why ASC 855 governs the measurement at December 31.
4. **far-subsequent-events-0016.** The ASU 2025-05 sentence is accurate but extra; it is the only stem in the batch that names an election that doesn't affect the answer. Keep it if you want to pin the expedient explicitly. Note that the receivable stays nonrecognized either way, because the regulation arose after year-end.
5. **far-change-in-estimate-0003 v2, choice D.** Two different errors produce D (new total life as divisor, or remaining life from the original life). That is harmless, but the rationale should name both.
